Rollcheck

A browser tool that checks a student spreadsheet against eligibility rules and sorts every student into Eligible, Not eligible or Needs review, each with a plain-English reason.

Approved Source devpost/scope.md (canonical) Hackathon Build With AI: Basics
01

The Unique Kernel

It refuses to guess.

Anything a formula would silently get wrong goes to Needs review with a sentence saying exactly why, instead of being quietly counted as pass or fail. Raw values are shown exactly as in the file, and the counts must always add up to the total rows.

02

Who It's For

Non-programmer university office staff (exam cells, scholarship sections, student records) who live in Excel.

Today they apply rules like "attendance at least 75%" or "fees cleared" to exported sheets by hand or with formulas, and the errors are silent. A wrong result decides whether a real student sits an exam or gets a scholarship.

03

The Core Loop

Repeated every exam or scholarship cycle with the next export, reusing a saved rule set.

04

What "Working" Looks Like

Click Try sample data (a public UCI dataset with documented traps), keep the demo rules absences is at most 10 AND G3 is at least 10, click Check students.

Counters

120 of 120 students checked, across three groups.

The moment that lands

Every hidden trap shows up in Needs review, each with its reason.

Proof

An .xlsx with Status and Reason columns plus a "Rules used" sheet.

Traps the demo should catch6
  • G3 = 9.6: within 1 of the 10 limit.
  • absences = "AB": not a number. Check manually.
  • A blank absences cell.
  • "9,8": comma decimal, not guessed.
  • "75%" / "10 ": number stored as text, parsed and flagged.
  • A duplicate ID, and the same student under a different spelling with matching age and school.
05

Inspiration & Identity

A well-kept office register: calm, precise, trustworthy. A serious tool, not an AI app. This page uses the same palette and type.

Wanted
  • IBM Plex Sans and Plex Mono, tabular numerals
  • Warm off-white paper, deep green accent
  • Thin 1px rules instead of shadows, 6px max radius
  • Short, calm, specific copy
Avoided
  • Purple gradients, glass, glow
  • Emoji, sparkle icons, "AI-powered" badges
  • Giant centred hero, heavy rounded cards
  • "Oops", exclamation marks, "magic"
06

Why This Matters to the Learner

"I work with student data at a university and see these mistakes happen."

Practice goal: building a fully tested logic core separately from the UI.

07

The POC Boundary

Larger than the course's 2–4 hour target, by the learner's choice. Kernel slices are built first so the demo works early. If time runs short, the remaining items move to Later openly, never left half-done.

Now In the proof of concept9
  • Upload .csv (delimiter auto-detected) or .xlsx (sheet picker); summary + 5-row preview; auto-detected ID and name columns.
  • 1–5 AND rules; six conditions; editable near-limit margin on number rules.
  • Pure, fully unit-tested rule engine separate from the UI, with the count invariant enforced.
  • Needs review detection: near-limit, text in number columns, blanks, duplicate IDs, likely duplicate students. Flag only.
  • Results: clickable counters, rule-impact summary, searchable table, row detail panel.
  • .xlsx export (results, Rules used, Needs review), save/load rule set as .json, print view.
  • Bundled sample data with pre-filled demo rules.
  • Runs entirely in the browser; 50,000+ rows stay responsive.
  • Light and dark mode; keyboard and screen-reader usable.
Later Worth doing, not now3
  • Saved rule-set library inside the app (beyond .json files).
  • Merging two files (e.g. attendance + fees exports).
  • OR-logic between rules.
Cut Explicitly out, with reasons5
  • AI features inside the app: the point is deterministic, explainable rules.
  • Login, accounts, server, database: the file must never leave the computer.
  • Scheduling: a manual, per-cycle chore.
  • Editing data in the app: the tool flags; the office fixes the source sheet.
  • Mobile-first layout: staff work at desktops; phones must not break, nothing more.
